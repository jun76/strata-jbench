港町の朝は、魚市場の競りの声から始まる。まだ空が群青色をしているうちに、漁船が一隻、また一隻と岸壁戻ってくる。船底に並んだ発泡スチロールの箱には、氷の鯵や鯖、季節外れの太刀魚が詰めされている。仲買人たちは長靴のsounds響 echoing as they peer into the boxes, raising two or three fingers to indicate prices. The exchange is rapid, barely audible to outsiders. Yet, as the auction concludes, the destination of each box is firmly decided.

The market's back alley houses a small diner. Sliding aside the curtain, steam rises from miso soup, and the day's most expensive catch, prepared as simmered fish, is served. Regular fisherman patrons sit shoulder-to-shoulder at the counter, discussing weather forecasts and the recent arrival of young crew members. The shop owner silently serves rice from a bowl, nodding briefly in response to conversation.

Past noon, the town abruptly quiets. The market floor is washed, leaving only gulls circling reluctantly over the quay. From the school atop the slope, children's voices during lunchtime drift on the wind. As evening approaches and the lighthouse lights kindle, the engines of the next fishing trip's vessels sputter to life one by one. Thus, the day of the harbor town passes, overlapping its end with its beginning.
