A city water supply is a complex, engineered system designed to deliver safe, potable water from natural sources to millions of consumers through a network of pipes, pumps, and treatment facilities. The journey from a river, lake, or underground aquifer to the kitchen tap involves several critical stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and quality monitoring. Each stage is essential for ensuring that the water is not only available but also safe for human consumption. Below is a detailed explanation of how this system works, using concrete examples to illustrate the processes.

### 1. Collection: The Source of Water

The process begins with the collection of raw water from natural sources. Cities typically rely on surface water (rivers, lakes, reservoirs) or groundwater (aquifers). The choice of source depends on geography, climate, and infrastructure availability.

**Surface Water Collection:**
In many large cities, surface water is the primary source. For example, New York City relies heavily on the Catskill and Delaware watersheds, which are reservoirs located in upstate New York. These reservoirs collect rainwater and runoff from surrounding forests and mountains. The water flows into large lakes or reservoirs, where it is stored before being transported to the city. In contrast, Los Angeles draws much of its water from the Colorado River via aqueducts, while also relying on local reservoirs like Lake Powell and Lake Mead.

**Groundwater Collection:**
Some cities, particularly in arid regions or areas with abundant underground water, rely on groundwater. Phoenix, Arizona, for instance, uses groundwater wells to supplement its supply. Groundwater is accessed by drilling wells into aquifers—underground layers of water-bearing permeable rock or unconsolidated materials. The water is pumped to the surface using electric pumps. Groundwater is often cleaner than surface water because it has been naturally filtered through soil and rock layers, but it can still contain minerals or contaminants that require treatment.

**Intake Structures:**
Regardless of the source, water is collected through intake structures. For surface water, these are large pipes or tunnels that draw water from the reservoir or river. For groundwater, wells are equipped with pumps and filtration screens to prevent debris from entering the system. The raw water is then transported via pipelines or aqueducts to treatment plants.
