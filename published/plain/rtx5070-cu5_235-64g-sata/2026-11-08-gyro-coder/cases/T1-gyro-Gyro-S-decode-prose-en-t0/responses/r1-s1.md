A city water supply is a complex, engineered system designed to deliver safe, potable water from natural sources to millions of consumers through a network of pipes, pumps, and treatment facilities. The journey from source to tap involves several critical stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and quality monitoring. Each stage is essential for ensuring that water is not only available but also safe for human consumption. Below is a detailed explanation of how this system works, using concrete examples to illustrate each phase.

### 1. Collection: The Source of Water

The process begins with the collection of raw water from natural sources. Cities typically rely on one or more of the following sources: surface water (rivers, lakes, reservoirs), groundwater (aquifers accessed via wells), or desalinated seawater in coastal regions.

**Concrete Example: Surface Water Collection**
Consider a city like Chicago, which draws its water from Lake Michigan. The collection process involves intake structures located near the shoreline. These intakes are designed to draw water from depths where the water is cooler and less susceptible to surface contaminants like algae blooms or debris. The water flows through large pipes into a pumping station. In Chicago, the water is pumped from the lake into a treatment plant located inland. The intake structures are equipped with screens to prevent large debris and aquatic organisms from entering the system.

**Concrete Example: Groundwater Collection**
In contrast, a city like Phoenix, Arizona, relies heavily on groundwater from the Salt River aquifer. Wells are drilled deep into the earth to access underground water reserves. These wells are equipped with pumps that lift the water to the surface. Groundwater collection requires careful monitoring to prevent over-extraction, which can lead to subsidence or depletion of the aquifer. In some cases, cities use "managed aquifer recharge," where excess surface water is intentionally injected into the ground to replenish groundwater supplies during wet seasons.
