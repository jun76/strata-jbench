The journey of a glass of water from a distant reservoir to the kitchen tap is one of the most complex, critical, and often invisible engineering feats in modern civilization. It is a continuous, high-stakes logistical operation that combines civil engineering, chemistry, biology, and data science. To understand how a city water supply works, we must trace the path of a single water molecule through six distinct stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and quality monitoring. This process ensures that the water arriving at your faucet is not only clear and odorless but also biologically safe and chemically stable.

### 1. Collection: The Source and Intake

The process begins at the source, which can be surface water (rivers, lakes, reservoirs) or groundwater (aquifers). For the sake of this explanation, let us consider a typical mid-sized city, "Riverton," which relies on a large surface reservoir located twenty miles upstream.

Collection starts at the intake structure. In Riverton, this is a submerged concrete tower extending into the reservoir. The design is crucial; intakes are often multi-level, allowing operators to draw water from different depths. This is vital because water quality varies by depth. Surface water may contain algae and debris, while deeper water is colder, clearer, and less oxygenated. By adjusting the intake valves, engineers can select the cleanest layer.

Before the water even enters the pipes, it passes through coarse screens. These are not fine filters but large grates designed to keep out fish, leaves, branches, and other large debris. In some advanced systems, like those in parts of the Netherlands or Singapore, pre-treatment might involve simple sedimentation basins where the water sits still for hours, allowing heavy particles to settle to the bottom before being pumped out. This initial step reduces the load on the downstream treatment plant, saving energy and chemicals.

### 2. Treatment: The Chemical and Physical Transformation

Once pumped from the intake, the raw water arrives at the treatment plant. Here, it undergoes a rigorous series of physical and chemical processes designed to remove contaminants and pathogens. This is the heart of the water supply system.

**Coagulation and Flocculation:** The first major step is coagulation. Raw water contains suspended particles that are too small to settle naturally.
