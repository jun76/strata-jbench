日本は位于温带至亚热带地区，且地形复杂性（从北海道到海南）以及海洋影响（黑洋 currents、季风等）使得四季具有独特性的深刻影响对社会 life、食文化、 and cultural practices. Unlike tropical or continental regions, Japan’s four seasons are not just meteorological phenomena but integral cultural frameworks. This section will explain how each season shapes human life, food culture, and annual events.

1. Spring: Renewal and Celebration

Life Influence: Spring marks the awakening of consciousness. Japanese society begins with New Year (January) as official holiday, but real “newness” comes in late spring (April-May). In March-April, people clean houses (“spring cleaning”), reflecting renewal philosophy.

Food Culture: Seasonal ingredients dominate.

- Seafood: First fish harvests include small mackerel and cod. Fresh river fish (whitebait) become delicacy.
- Vegetables: Wild greens like wild onions, bamboo shoots, and bamboo sprouts emerge from mountain forests. Bamboo shoots are iconic spring symbol, used in “bamboo shoot curry.”
- Fruit: Cherry and plum blossoms are not just visual; they signal start of fruiting, leading to early cherries and plums.
- Traditional Dishes: Spring festivals include “cherry blossom viewing” food, often including rice cakes, tea, and wild vegetables. “Spring vegetable stir-fries” emphasize freshness, not preservation.
- Drinks: “Spring wine” refers to early harvest, light-bodied rice wines paired with fresh seafood and greens.

Annual Events: Japanese cherry blossoms bloom March-May, becoming focal event.

- Cherry Blossom Viewing: Major annual festival. People gather under blooming cherry trees, with food and drink, embodying celebration of natural beauty.

- April 22 Earth Day: Marks start of spring, with tree-planting activities.
- May Day Labour Day: Often celebrated with spring-themed parties.

2. Summer: Intense Heat, Humidity and Festivals

Life Influence: Summer is Japan’s peak cultural period. Humidity and heat create challenges, but also enable agriculture.

- Agriculture: Rice cultivation becomes main labor during summer, requiring flooded fields and high temperatures. Rice planting and harvesting are labor-intensive, centralizing labor.

- Clothing: Cotton and linen clothing become standard, due to heat. Traditional costumes like “kimonos” were worn year-round, but modern fashion adapted.
