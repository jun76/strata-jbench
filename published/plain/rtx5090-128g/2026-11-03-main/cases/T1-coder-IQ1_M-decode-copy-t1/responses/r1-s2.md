港町の朝は、魚市場の競りの声から始まる。まだ空が群青色をしているうちに、漁船が一隻、また一隻と岸壁戻ってくる。船底に並んだ発泡スチロールの箱には、氷と一緒に鯵や鯖、時には季節外れの太刀魚が詰めわれている。仲買人たちは長靴の音を響かせながら箱をのぞき込み、指を二本、三本と立てて値がつける。そのやりがは早口で、外から来た人はほとんど聞き取はれない。但是,競りがはの終わる頃には, which箱がwhich店へgoes的がはproperly determined.

市场のback has a small食堂there. When you pass through the暖 curtain, soup of a boiling miso soup and that day's first catch of the fish is served. The regular fishermen, who are on the counter shoulder to shoulder, talk about the weather map and the newly hired young crewman. The owner silently serves rice into bowls and occasionally nods briefly to the conversation.

After noon, the town suddenly becomes quiet. The floor of the market is washed with water, and seagulls are circling over the pier sadly. On the slope leading to the elementary school, during the lunch break, children's voices can be heard carried by the wind. In the evening, when the lights of the lighthouse light up, one by one, the engines of the next fishing boats begin to run. Thus, a day of a port town ends and begins overlappingly while passing.
