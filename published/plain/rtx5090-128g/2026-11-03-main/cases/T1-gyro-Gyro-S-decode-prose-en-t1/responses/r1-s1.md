A city water supply system is one of the most complex and essential pieces of infrastructure in modern urban life. It transforms raw, natural water from rivers, lakes, or underground aquifers into a safe, potable resource delivered to homes, businesses, and public facilities. This journey involves several distinct stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and continuous quality monitoring. To understand how a tap delivers clean water, we must trace the entire pathway from the source to the end-user, using concrete examples from cities like New York, Chicago, and Seattle to illustrate the process.

**1. Collection: The Source and Intake**

The journey begins at the water source, which can be surface water (rivers, lakes) or groundwater (aquifers). Surface water collection typically involves large intake structures designed to draw water while minimizing debris and contamination. For example, the City of New York relies on reservoirs in the Catskill Mountains, such as the Ashokan Reservoir. Water from these reservoirs flows through aqueducts—massive tunnels or pipelines—due to gravity. Because the reservoirs are located at higher elevations than the city, gravity provides the initial flow without requiring pumps for long distances. This is known as a gravity-fed system, which is energy-efficient and reliable.

Groundwater collection involves wells drilled into aquifers. Cities like Houston, Texas, rely heavily on groundwater. However, surface water systems often use a hybrid approach. Many municipalities have backup wells for emergency situations when surface water sources are compromised by drought or contamination. The intake points are strategically placed away from potential pollution sources, such as agricultural runoff zones or industrial discharge points. Screening devices, such as coarse screens and fine screens, remove large debris like branches, plastic, and fish before water enters the treatment facility. This initial filtration prevents damage to pumps and pipelines and reduces the load on downstream treatment processes.

**2. Treatment: Purification and Safety**

Once collected, water undergoes rigorous treatment to meet public health standards. The treatment process varies depending on the source water quality but generally follows a standardized sequence: coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection.
