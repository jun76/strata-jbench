A city water supply is a complex, engineered system designed to deliver safe, potable water from natural sources to millions of consumers through a network of pipes, pumps, and treatment facilities. The journey from a river or aquifer to a kitchen tap involves several critical stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and quality monitoring. Each stage is essential for ensuring that the water is not only available but also safe for human consumption. Below is a detailed explanation of how this system works, using concrete examples to illustrate the processes.

### 1. Collection: The Source of Water

The process begins with the collection of raw water from natural sources. Cities typically rely on two primary sources: surface water (rivers, lakes, and reservoirs) and groundwater (aquifers).

**Surface Water Collection:**
Many large cities, such as New York City, rely on surface water. New York’s water supply comes from the Catskill and Delaware watersheds, which are reservoirs located hundreds of miles away. These reservoirs collect rain and snowmelt, which flows into rivers and lakes. The water is then transported via aqueducts—large pipelines or tunnels—to the city. For example, the Catskill Aqueduct is a 120-mile-long tunnel that carries water from the Ashokan Reservoir to Brooklyn. This method requires significant infrastructure to move water over long distances, but it provides a consistent supply.

**Groundwater Collection:**
Smaller cities or those in arid regions often rely on groundwater. Groundwater is accessed through wells drilled into aquifers—underground layers of water-bearing permeable rock or unconsolidated materials. For instance, Phoenix, Arizona, relies heavily on groundwater from the Salt River aquifer. Wells are drilled deep into the ground, and pumps extract the water. Groundwater is often cleaner than surface water because it is naturally filtered through soil and rock layers, but it can be vulnerable to contamination from industrial activities or agricultural runoff.
