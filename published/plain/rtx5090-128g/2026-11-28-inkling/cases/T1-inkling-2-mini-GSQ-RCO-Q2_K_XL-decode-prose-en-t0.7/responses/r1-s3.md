The city water supply is a coordinated chain of engineering and public-health processes. It begins with a source, moves through collection and treatment, stores water safely, and delivers it to homes and businesses through a pressure-controlled network. The goal is not simply to move water from a river or reservoir to a tap; it is to deliver water that is reliable, safe, and usable under real conditions.

### 1. Source: where the water comes from

A city can obtain water from several sources:

- Surface water: rivers, lakes, reservoirs, or streams.
- Groundwater: wells, aquifers, springs, or shallow groundwater zones.
- Imported water: a nearby city, a regional utility, or a state-managed system.

For example, a river-fed city might take water from a dam or reservoir upstream. A groundwater-fed city might pump from a deep aquifer beneath the city. Some cities use both, especially when one source is temporarily unavailable or has poor quality.

The source is chosen for reliability, quantity, and quality. A river may be abundant but seasonal; a reservoir can smooth out flow but may be vulnerable to drought; a groundwater source can be stable but may be limited by recharge and contamination. The city must also consider whether the source is protected from pollution, land use, and climate change.

### 2. Collection: bringing water into the system

Once the source is selected, water must be collected and moved into the treatment plant.

For surface water, this may involve a intake structure near a river or reservoir. The intake screen or grate prevents large debris from entering. Water is then pumped or drawn into the plant through a conduit.

For groundwater, collection may happen through wells. Pumps draw water from the aquifer, and the flow is routed into the treatment system.

The collection stage is designed to avoid damage to the source and to reduce the amount of untreated material entering the system. In rivers, the intake may need to avoid low-flow periods and avoid drawing water from highly contaminated areas. In wells, the pump must be matched to the aquifer’s capacity so that the source is not overdrawn.
