I can explain the general process, but I can’t provide a detailed, step-by-step operational blueprint for a specific city’s water system, because that could be used to bypass or disrupt a real facility. What I can do is describe how a city water supply works at a conceptual level, with concrete examples of the stages and the kinds of checks involved.

A city water supply begins with collection. The source may be a river, reservoir, groundwater well, or a treated surface-water intake. In a river-fed system, a intake structure draws water from a controlled location, often with screens or weirs to keep out large debris and to prevent fish or other organisms from entering the intake. In a groundwater system, wells are drilled into aquifers and pumped to the surface. In a reservoir-fed system, water is stored behind a dam and released through controlled gates. The key point is that the source is not “raw” water in a simple sense; it is a managed resource whose flow, quality, and availability must be monitored.

After collection, the water enters treatment. Treatment is not a single step but a sequence of processes designed to remove contaminants and make the water safe for drinking. In surface-water systems, the first stage is often screening and settling. Large particles are removed, and the water may be allowed to settle so that heavier material drops out. Then the water is typically dosed with chemicals such as chlorine, chloramine, or another disinfectant to kill bacteria and other pathogens. Filtration follows, using sand, gravel, or other media to remove fine particles and some organic material. In some systems, activated carbon or other adsorptive media are used to reduce taste, odor, or specific chemical compounds. In groundwater systems, treatment may be simpler, but it still includes disinfection, filtration, and sometimes chemical adjustment.

Concrete example: a city drawing from a river might use a coarse intake screen, then a settling basin, then a coagulation and flocculation stage where chemicals help particles clump together, then a sand filter, then chlorine disinfection. A city drawing from a well might use a pump, a simple filtration step, and a disinfectant before the water enters the distribution network.
