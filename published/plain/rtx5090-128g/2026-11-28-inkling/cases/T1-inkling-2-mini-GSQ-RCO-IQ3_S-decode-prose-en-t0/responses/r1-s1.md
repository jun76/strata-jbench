A city water supply system is a coordinated chain of infrastructure that moves water from a natural source to homes, businesses, and public buildings. The process begins with collection, where water is drawn from a river, lake, reservoir, groundwater well, or treated surface source. In many cities, the source is not used directly; it is first screened and pumped into a treatment plant. For example, a city near a river may install intake structures with screens to keep out large debris such as leaves, branches, and fish. A city relying on groundwater may instead use wells drilled into aquifers, with pumps and casings designed to prevent contamination.

Once collected, the water enters treatment. The exact steps vary by source and local regulations, but a typical surface-water plant includes screening, coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection. Screening removes large objects. Coagulation adds chemicals such as aluminum sulfate or ferric chloride to help tiny particles clump together. Flocculation mixes the water gently so those particles form larger clusters. Sedimentation allows the clusters to settle out in basins. Filtration then passes the water through sand, gravel, or other media to remove remaining fine particles. Disinfection, often with chlorine, chloramine, or ultraviolet light, kills harmful microorganisms. Some systems also add fluoride or adjust pH to improve safety and taste. Groundwater systems may require less treatment, but they still need testing and sometimes disinfection or iron and manganese removal.

After treatment, the water is stored. Storage tanks, reservoirs, and elevated towers provide a buffer between treatment and demand. During peak morning or evening use, the system can draw from stored water while pumps continue to treat and supply more. Storage also helps maintain pressure and protects against short-term interruptions. A city might use a large underground reservoir near the treatment plant, or several smaller tanks distributed across neighborhoods. In some places, a hilltop tower stores water under gravity, allowing it to flow downhill into homes without constant pumping.

Distribution is the network of pipes that carries treated water from storage to users. The system is usually divided into trunk mains, secondary lines, and service connections. Trunk mains carry large volumes across the city, while smaller pipes branch into streets and neighborhoods. Service connections link the public network to individual homes and buildings.
