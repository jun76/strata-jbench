A municipal water supply generally moves through six stages:

1. Source collection
- Rivers, lakes, reservoirs, groundwater wells, or treated surface water are used.
- Cities may draw from one or several sources to balance supply and risk.
- Example: a city might use a reservoir for steady supply and wells for backup during dry periods.

2. Treatment
- Raw water is screened for large debris and often pumped to a plant.
- Treatment can include coagulation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection.
- In some systems, chlorination or other disinfectants are used; in others, UV or advanced oxidation may play a role.
- Example: a river-fed plant may remove sediment and then use chlorination to kill bacteria and pathogens.

3. Storage
- Treated water is stored in reservoirs or tanks to even out demand.
- Storage buffers peak morning use, firefighting needs, and short-term supply drops.
- Example: a city’s elevated storage tank keeps pressure higher on the second floor of a building.

4. Distribution
- Pipes carry water from the plant to homes, schools, hospitals, and businesses.
- Distribution is often arranged in loops or networks to improve redundancy.
- Example: a dense neighborhood may use smaller branch lines fed from a main trunk line.

5. Pressure management
- Pressure is controlled by pumps, valves, pressure zones, and tanks.
- This helps protect pipes, reduce leakage, and prevent low-pressure service in tall buildings.
- Example: a city may use multiple pressure zones so a hillside area has enough flow without straining the system.

6. Quality monitoring
- Routine sampling checks turbidity, bacteria, alkalinity, hardness, residual chlorine, pH, and other indicators.
- Cities also test for taste, odor, and contaminants tied to local sources.
- Example: a lab may check a well every few weeks and verify that chlorine remains effective along the distribution line.

In practice, the system is also maintained with metering, flushing, leak repair, pressure regulation, and emergency isolation valves so one problem does not shut down the whole network.
