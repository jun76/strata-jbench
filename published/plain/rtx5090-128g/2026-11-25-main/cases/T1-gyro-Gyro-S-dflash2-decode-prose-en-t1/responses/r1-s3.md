A city’s water supply system is a marvel of modern civil engineering, representing the intricate journey a molecule of water takes from a distant source to the sink in a residential kitchen. This process is not merely about moving water; it is a complex chain of operations designed to ensure safety, consistency, availability, and pressure stability. To understand this fully, we must trace the path through six critical stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and quality monitoring. Each stage relies on specific technologies and principles, often illustrated by real-world examples such as those seen in cities like New York, San Francisco, or London.

### 1. Collection: The Source and Intake

The journey begins at the source. Depending on the geography and infrastructure of the city, the water may originate from groundwater wells, rivers, lakes, or reservoirs. For instance, New York City relies heavily on reservoirs upstate, such as the Croton system, while Las Vegas depends on groundwater pumping and imported water from Lake Mead via the Colorado River.

Collection involves **intake facilities**, which are engineering structures designed to draw raw water from the source into the treatment plant. In river systems, intakes are often submerged structures with screens that prevent debris, leaves, and aquatic life from entering the pipes. In reservoir systems, intakes are positioned to draw from different depths to optimize temperature and turbidity. For groundwater, wells are drilled into aquifers, and pumps draw the water to the surface.

A crucial aspect of collection is **pre-screening**. Before water even enters the main pipeline, large debris like branches or trash is removed. In many systems, a preliminary grit chamber or coarse screen is used to protect downstream pumps and equipment. This is vital because raw water often contains sediments and organic matter that could damage treatment infrastructure. For example, in the Thames Water system in London, river intakes have automated screens that periodically clean themselves, ensuring continuous flow without manual intervention.

### 2. Treatment: Purification and Safety

Once collected, raw water is transported to a **water treatment plant**. This is where the transformation from potentially unsafe raw water to potable drinking water occurs. Treatment is a multi-step process designed to remove contaminants, pathogens, and particulates.
