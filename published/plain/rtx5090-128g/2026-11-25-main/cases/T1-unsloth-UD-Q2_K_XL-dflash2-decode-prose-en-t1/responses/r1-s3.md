The journey of a glass of clean, cold water from the tap in a modern urban kitchen is one of the most complex, reliable, and critical engineering feats in human history. It is a system that seamlessly blends civil engineering, chemical science, hydraulic physics, and public health policy. To understand how a city water supply works, we must trace the water’s path through several distinct stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, pressure management, and quality monitoring. This narrative will explore these stages in detail, using concrete examples to illustrate the technical realities that ensure public safety.

### Stage 1: Collection and Intake

The process begins at the source, which for most cities is either a surface water body (such as a lake, river, or reservoir) or an aquifer (an underground layer of water-bearing permeable rock). The choice of source depends on geography, population density, and historical infrastructure.

For surface water, cities often construct intake structures far from the shore to avoid sediment and contaminants that accumulate near the banks. A classic example is New York City, which draws its water from the Catskill and Delaware watersheds. These are largely unfiltered reservoirs, protected by strict land-use regulations. The water enters the city via gravity-fed aqueducts, massive tunnels and pipes that rely on elevation differences rather than pumps, saving enormous amounts of energy.

In contrast, cities like Los Angeles or Phoenix often rely on a mix of surface water and deep groundwater wells. When the source is a river, such as the Colorado River serving Las Vegas, intake facilities use large screens to prevent fish and debris from entering the system. These intakes are often submerged and positioned at depths where the water is cooler and less likely to contain algae blooms, which are common in warmer, shall areas. The raw water then travels through large-diameter transmission mains, often ranging from 40 to 100 inches in diameter, toward the treatment plant.

### Stage 2: Water Treatment

Once the raw water arrives at the treatment plant, it undergoes a rigorous multi-step purification process. The goal is to remove physical particles, biological pathogens, and dissolved contaminants. The specific method varies depending on the source quality, but the general sequence involves coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, filtration, and disinfection.
