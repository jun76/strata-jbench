A city water supply is a chain of systems that moves water from a natural or engineered source to the tap in a home, school, hospital, or business. The chain usually includes **source water collection**, **treatment**, **storage**, **distribution through pipes**, **pressure management**, and **continuous quality monitoring**. Although every city’s system is different, the basic goal is the same: provide enough safe water at sufficient pressure, every hour of every day.

Below is a detailed step-by-step explanation of how a typical municipal water supply works, with concrete examples.

---

## 1. Source water collection: where the water comes from

A city’s water supply begins with a **source**. The source may be a river, lake, reservoir, groundwater aquifer, spring, or even seawater if the city uses desalination.

### Common source types

**Surface water** comes from rivers, lakes, and reservoirs. Many large cities rely on surface water because it can provide large volumes. For example, a city might draw water from a river upstream of the urban area, then treat it before sending it into the distribution network.

**Groundwater** comes from underground aquifers. Water is pumped from wells. This is common in smaller cities or areas where underground water is abundant. Groundwater often has fewer visible impurities than river water, but it can contain dissolved minerals such as iron, calcium, arsenic, or nitrate.

**Reservoirs** are artificial lakes created by dams. They collect rainfall and river flow, storing water for dry seasons. A city reservoir may supply millions of residents. For example, if a region has heavy rain in winter and little rain in summer, the reservoir fills during the wet season and releases water gradually during the dry season.

**Desalination** is used in coastal cities where freshwater is scarce. Seawater is treated to remove salt and minerals. This is energy-intensive but can provide a reliable supply in places such as coastal cities in arid regions.

### Collection structures

Once the source is identified, water is collected through infrastructure such as:

- **Intake pipes** placed in rivers or lakes  
- **Dams and reservoirs** that store surface water  
- **Wells** that pump groundwater  
- **Pumping stations** that lift water from lower elevations or underground sources  
- **Raw water pipelines** that carry untreated water to treatment plants
