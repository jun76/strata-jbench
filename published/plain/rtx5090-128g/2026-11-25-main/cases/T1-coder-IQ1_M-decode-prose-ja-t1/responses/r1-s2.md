日本における四季の循環（春、夏、秋、冬）は、不仅仅是 meteorological changes but a fundamental pillar that shapes the Japanese way of life, culinary culture, and annual customs. Unlike many other regions where climate remains relatively stable year-round, Japan’s distinct four seasons trigger a synchronized shift in daily habits, dietary preferences, emotional responses, and social rituals. This phenomenon, often referred to as “seasonal harmony (季節 harmony),” creates a unique rhythm of existence for humans, reflecting both geographical characteristics and cultural traditions developed over centuries. Below, detailed explanations with abundant concrete examples are provided.

**1. Spring (March to May): Rebirth and Awakening**

Spring marks the beginning of the year in Japanese cultural perception. It is the season of awakening nature and the start of agricultural cycles.

*   **Impact on Human Life:** The most significant psychological shift occurs here. After the long, cold winter, the arrival of spring brings an intense feeling of “renewal.” People begin to open their windows and doors widely, anticipating fresh air. This season also triggers intense emotional reactions due to the sudden temperature rise. Japanese people frequently experience “sudden warmth,” leading them to hastily pack away heavy winter coats and switch to lighter layers. Furthermore, the allergy season (especially pollen from cypress trees and birch trees) causes many people to sneeze or wear masks, which has become a cultural topic of humor and annoyance in urban areas.
*   **Food Culture:** Spring cuisine is defined by freshness and the symbolic representation of renewal. Key seasonal ingredients include:
    *   **Asparagus (Sparrowgrass):** Fresh green asparagus, often served grilled with olive oil or in pasta, symbolizes the arrival of spring.
    *   **Spring Onion (Green Onion) and Fava Beans:** Fava beans are highly seasonal, appearing only in late spring. Stir-fried with ham or served raw with salt, they represent a specific culinary celebration limited to this period.
    *   **Strawberry Festival:** The strawberry harvest in Aichi Prefecture (Miyoshi) or Kagoshima marks a major regional custom. Strawberries, red as a symbol of “awakening,” are consumed in vast quantities during festivals.
