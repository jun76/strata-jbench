The journey of a single glass of water from a distant reservoir to a kitchen tap is one of the most complex, energy-intensive, and critical feats of modern civil engineering. It is a system that operates silently in the background of daily life, yet its failure can halt the function of a metropolis. To understand this process, we must trace the path of water through five distinct stages: collection, treatment, storage, distribution, and the final delivery at the consumer’s tap. Each stage involves rigorous scientific protocols, massive infrastructure investments, and constant monitoring to ensure safety and reliability.

**1. Collection: The Source and Intake**

The journey begins at the source, which is typically a surface water body such as a lake, river, or reservoir, though groundwater from deep aquifers is also common. For a major city like Chicago, the source is Lake Michigan, a vast body of freshwater that serves as a natural reservoir. In contrast, cities like Phoenix or Las Vegas rely heavily on Colorado River water or deep wells.

The collection process starts at an intake structure. This is not simply a pipe sticking into the water; it is a sophisticated facility designed to prevent debris, aquatic life, and heavy pollutants from entering the system. For example, intake pipes often extend hundreds of feet into the lake or river, positioned below the surface where the water is colder, clearer, and less prone to algal blooms that occur near the shore in warm months. At the mouth of these pipes, coarse screens and bar racks act as the first line of defense, trapping logs, plastic bags, and large debris. In some advanced systems, fine mesh screens are used to exclude small fish and plankton.

Once inside the intake chamber, the water is often chlorinated immediately. This pre-chlorination serves a dual purpose: it prevents the growth of bacteria and biofilm inside the long transmission pipes that carry raw water to the treatment plant, and it helps control the growth of zebra mussels in certain regions, which can clog infrastructure. The raw water is then pumped through large-diameter transmission mains—sometimes as wide as 12 feet in diameter—traveling miles to reach the water treatment facility.
