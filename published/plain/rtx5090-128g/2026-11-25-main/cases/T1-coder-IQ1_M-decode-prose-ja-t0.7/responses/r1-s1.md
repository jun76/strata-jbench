### 1. Spring (March-May): Rebirth and Festivals
Spring is the season of awakening, marked by the blooming of cherry blossoms and the harvest of new crops. It significantly impacts both daily life and cultural celebrations.

**Influences on Daily Life:**
In spring, people begin to prioritize "change of season" habits. After enduring the cold of winter, the increase in sunlight prompts people to open windows, change to lighter bedding, and engage in outdoor activities such as walking in parks and cycling. The high pollen levels in spring (especially birch and cypress pollen) cause some people to wear masks to avoid allergic reactions, which is a distinctive characteristic of Japanese spring life. Additionally, the return of migratory birds and blooming flowers become focal points for photography and nature walks.

**Food Culture:**
Spring is the season for "spring greens" (spring vegetables). Examples include:
- **Asparagus and Bamboo Shoots:** Spring bamboo shoots are symbolic of spring, used in dishes like "bamboo shoot rice" and "bamboo shoot stir-fry."
- **Strawberries:** Spring is the peak season for strawberries, often enjoyed as "strawberry shortcake" or "strawberry milkshakes."
- **Morel and Wild Vegetables:** People gather wild vegetables like wild garlic, shepherd's purse, and nettles.
- **Seafood:** Spring is the peak season for oysters and squid, used in dishes like "grilled oysters" and "squid stir-fry."

**Annual Rituals:**
- **Hakone Spring Festival:** A celebration of local agricultural products and arts.
- **Cherry Blossom Viewing (Sakura Matsuri):** This is the most iconic spring ritual. From Tokyo's cherry blossom avenue to the Hakone flower fields, cherry blossoms become cultural symbols. People hold picnics under the trees and take group photos.
